Storage guide

Protect the ingredient before you cook it

Good storage is part of good cooking. Keep seafood cold, organized and planned around the meal you intend to make.

Keep it cold

Move seafood into the coldest practical part of the refrigerator and avoid leaving it at room temperature while you prep other ingredients.

Plan the cooking window

Use your order timing as a guide and prioritize seafood dishes when the ingredient is at its best rather than letting it wait unnecessarily.

Keep surfaces clean

Use clean boards, knives and containers, then wash and dry preparation surfaces promptly after handling raw seafood.

Cooking confidence

Let texture guide your technique

Delicate white fish rewards gentle heat, while firmer species can take stronger searing, grilling or longer curry cooking. Prawns are quick and expressive, so the main risk is overcooking rather than under-seasoning.

Use our product notes as a starting point, then adjust heat and timing to the thickness of the portion you actually have.

Ingredient planning

Build the plate around the seafood

Once you know the fish, choose a simple supporting structure of starch, vegetables, herbs and acidity.

Choose a base

Rice, noodles, flatbread or potatoes can turn a seafood preparation into a complete meal.

Add freshness

Greens, herbs, citrus and crisp vegetables bring contrast to richer fish and shellfish.

Finish with brightness

A fresh sauce, lemon, pickle or herb oil can lift the final plate without covering the seafood.
